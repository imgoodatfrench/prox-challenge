# Welding Tips

A good way to test welding technique is to examine a weld's appearance after it has cooled and the slag has been removed.  Then, better welding can be learned by adjusting your weld technique to remedy any problems found.

**NOTICE:** TIG welding is a complicated process, requiring experience and skill to achieve successful results. Training beyond the scope of this manual is required to TIG weld properly.

> **After practice welding a couple of welding beads, STOP and examine your weld using the following guidelines.**

## Strike Test

A test weld on a **PIECE OF SCRAP** can be tested by using the following procedure. **WEAR ANSI-APPROVED SAFETY GOGGLES DURING THIS PROCEDURE.**

<u>**CAUTION!**</u>  This test **WILL** damage the weld it is performed on.  This test is **ONLY** an indicator of weld technique and is not intended to test working welds.

1. After two scraps have been welded together and the weld has cooled, clamp one scrap in a sturdy vise.

2. Stay clear from underneath while you strike the opposite scrap with a heavy hammer, preferably a dead-blow hammer.

3. A **GOOD WELD** will deform but not break, as shown on top.
   A **POOR WELD** will be brittle and snap at the weld, as shown on bottom.

> **[Strike Test (top) — diagram]** Illustration of the good-weld outcome of the strike test. A SCRAP workpiece is held horizontally in a clamp (vise) at the right; two pieces are joined by a weld just left of the clamp jaws. A dead-blow hammer swings downward (arrow pointing down) onto the free end of the scrap at the left; impact lines show the strike. The struck scrap is bent downward at an angle at the weld but remains attached. Data: Callouts — "dead-blow hammer" (left), "clamp" (upper right), "SCRAP workpiece" (center right). Caption: "GOOD WELD bends and is not brittle."

> **[Strike Test (bottom) — diagram]** Illustration of the poor-weld outcome. Same setup: SCRAP workpiece held in a clamp at right, dead-blow hammer striking downward (arrow) on the free scrap at left with impact lines. Here the struck scrap has separated completely at the weld joint and fallen away below, leaving a clean break. Data: Callouts — "dead-blow hammer" (left), "clamp" (upper right), "SCRAP workpiece" (center right). Caption: "POOR WELD snaps or cracks."

## Cleaning the Weld

> **⚠ WARNING**
>
> **TO PREVENT SERIOUS INJURY:**
> Continue to wear ANSI-approved safety goggles and protective wear when cleaning a weld.
> Sparks or chips may fly when cleaning.

1. A weld from flux-cored wire welding or stick welding will be covered by slag.  Use a chipping hammer to knock this off.  **Be careful not to damage the weld or base material.**

2. Use a wire brush to further clean the weld or use an angle grinder (sold separately) to shape the weld.

Data: Callouts — "Chipping Hammer" (pointing to the pointed head at top) and "Wire Brush" (pointing to the bristle row along the lower body).
