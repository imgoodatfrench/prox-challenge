# IMPORTANT

**Securely hold onto the end of the welding wire and keep tension on it during the following steps.**

**If this is not done, the welding wire will unravel and unspool which can cause tangling and feeding problems.**

---

18. Cut off all bent and crimped wire. The cut end must have no burrs or sharp edges; cut again if needed.

19. Keep tension on the wire and guide at least 12 inches of wire into the Wire Inlet Liner and Feed Guide.

> **[Figure — diagram]** Line drawing of a hand pulling welding wire off the Wire Spool and feeding it into the wire feed mechanism. Data: Callouts — "HOLD WIRE SECURELY" (pointing at the gloved hand gripping the wire), "Welding Wire" (the wire coming off the spool), "Wire Spool" (labeled on the spool body, with a dashed arrow around the spool indicating the unwinding/rotation direction, arrow pointing clockwise toward the wire exit at upper right), "Wire Inlet Liner" (the short tube the wire enters at the rear of the feed mechanism), and "Feed Guide" (inside the feed mechanism housing, downstream of the inlet liner). The wire path runs from the spool, through the hand, into the Wire Inlet Liner, and on into the Feed Guide of the drive assembly.

20. Make sure the welding wire is resting in the groove of the Feed Roller, then push the wire Idler Arm down, and swing the Feed Tensioner up to latch it across the tip of the arm.
    <u>After</u> the wire is held by the Tensioner, you may release it.

<u>**Note:**</u> The tension should be 3–5 for solid wire and 2–3 for flux-cored wire. Too much force on flux-cored wire will crush it and may cause feeding issues.

> **[Figure — diagram]** Two-part (before/after) line drawing of the wire drive assembly with a large black arrow pointing from the left view to the right view. Data: Left view callout "Idler Arm" with a curved arrow showing the arm being pushed DOWN onto the wire and a second curved arrow at the lower left showing the tensioner swinging UP. Right view callout "Feed Tensioner" showing the knurled tension knob/tensioner latched upright across the tip of the idler arm (the closed/latched position). Both views show the feed roller and drive housing marked "VULCAN".

21. Pull the Nozzle to remove it.

22. Unscrew the Contact Tip counterclockwise and remove.

23. Lay the MIG Gun Cable out in a straight line so that the welding wire moves through it easily. Leave the cover open, so that the feed mechanism can be observed.

> **[Figure — diagram]** Line drawing of the MIG gun with its front-end consumables pulled apart/exploded to the left of the gun neck. Data: Callouts — "MIG Gun" (the gun body/handle with trigger and cable strain relief), "Nozzle" (the outermost cylindrical shroud, shown pulled straight off the front, at far left), and "Contact Tip" (the threaded tip between the nozzle and the gun neck/diffuser). Order from front to back: Nozzle → Contact Tip → gun neck → gun handle → cable.

# IMPORTANT

Stainless steel wire is less flexible than other welding wire. Therefore, it is more difficult to feed through the liner and gun. It is especially important to keep the gun cable straight while feeding stainless steel wire.
