# HOW TO CHOOSE A WELDER

> **IMPORTANT!** IDENTIFY YOUR INPUT VOLTAGE: DO YOU HAVE 120 VOLT OR 240 VOLT?

## Selection Decision Chart

The chart is organized as six numbered decision rows across four process columns. The highlighted (red) cell in row 1 indicates the skill level that applies to each process.

| # | Question | FLUX-CORED / FCAW | MIG / GMAW | STICK / SMAW | TIG / GTAW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **WHAT IS YOUR SKILL LEVEL?** | **LOW** (highlighted) / MODERATE / HIGH | **LOW** (highlighted) / MODERATE / HIGH | LOW / **MODERATE** (highlighted) / HIGH | LOW / MODERATE / **HIGH** (highlighted) |
| **2** | **WILL YOU NEED SHIELDING GAS?** | **NO-GAS REQUIRED** | **GAS REQUIRED** — *Indoor welding recommended* | **NO-GAS REQUIRED** | **GAS REQUIRED** — *Indoor welding recommended* |
| **3** | **WHAT TYPE OF MATERIAL WILL YOU BE WELDING?** | STEEL, STAINLESS STEEL | STEEL, STAINLESS STEEL, ALUMINUM *(Spool gun required)* | STEEL, STAINLESS STEEL, CASTINGS | STEEL, STAINLESS STEEL, CHROME MOLY → **DC TIG REQUIRED**; ALUMINUM, MAGNESIUM ALLOYS → **AC TIG REQUIRED** |
| **4** | **WHAT IS YOUR MATERIAL THICKNESS?** | 18 Gauge to 5/16" | 22 Gauge to 3/8" | 10 Gauge to 1/2" | 24 Gauge to 3/16" |
| **5** | **TYPICAL APPLICATIONS** | • Galvanized steel<br>• Pipe and tubing<br>• General fabrication<br>• Maintenance & repair | • Sheet metal<br>• Tubing<br>• Automotive body<br>• Structural steel | • Pipe and tubing<br>• Pressure vessels<br>• Structural steel<br>• Maintenance & repair | • Stainless steel exhausts<br>• Thin wall pipe & tubing<br>• Bicycle frames<br>• Metal art |
| **6** | **HOW CLEAN DO YOU NEED YOUR WELD?** | MORE SPATTER | CLEAN / MINIMAL SPATTER | MORE SPATTER | EXTREMELY CLEAN |
| ➜ | **USE THIS WELDING PROCESS** | **FLUX-CORED / FCAW**<br>• Ideal for outdoor or windy conditions<br>• Forgiving on rusty or dirty steels<br>• Good out of position welding capabilities<br>• High deposition rates achievable | **MIG / GMAW**<br>• Fast production (high welding speeds)<br>• Easiest to learn<br>• Clean welds with no slag<br>• Better control on thin materials | **STICK / SMAW**<br>• Ideal for outdoor or windy conditions<br>• Forgiving on rusty or dirty steels<br>• Deep penetration<br>• Good choice for thicker materials | **TIG / GTAW**<br>• Highest quality welds<br>• Extremely aesthetic weld appearance<br>• Can be used on a variety of materials<br>• Precise control |

> **[Row 6 weld bead photos — photo]** Row 6 ("HOW CLEAN DO YOU NEED YOUR WELD?") contains four close-up photographs of finished weld beads, one per process column, illustrating relative weld cleanliness. Data: Flux-Cored column — a rough, discolored (brown/rust-toned) bead labeled "MORE SPATTER"; MIG column — a bright, uniform bead with tight ripples labeled "CLEAN / MINIMAL SPATTER"; Stick column — an irregular bead with visible slag/spatter labeled "MORE SPATTER"; TIG column — a highly uniform bead of evenly stacked, coin-like ripples labeled "EXTREMELY CLEAN".

> **[Process icons — diagram]** Each process cell in the bottom row is preceded by a small orange square icon depicting the process. Data: Flux-Cored/FCAW and MIG/GMAW show a welding gun/torch icon; Stick/SMAW shows an electrode holder with stick electrode; TIG/GTAW shows a TIG torch with arc.

> **[Selection logic — chart]** Decision flow of the chart. Data: The user reads down the six numbered questions and matches their answers to a column; the arrow labeled "USE THIS WELDING PROCESS" at the bottom left points right into the four process result boxes. Low skill + no gas + steel/stainless + 18 ga–5/16" + more spatter acceptable → Flux-Cored/FCAW. Low skill + gas required (indoor) + steel/stainless/aluminum (spool gun) + 22 ga–3/8" + clean/minimal spatter → MIG/GMAW. Moderate skill + no gas + steel/stainless/castings + 10 ga–1/2" + more spatter acceptable → Stick/SMAW. High skill + gas required (indoor) + steel/stainless/chrome moly (DC TIG) or aluminum/magnesium (AC TIG) + 24 ga–3/16" + extremely clean → TIG/GTAW.

## The Duty Cycle

**The Duty Cycle** is the number of minutes, within a 10 minute period, a welding process can produce the specified welding current without overheating.

### DUTY CYCLE EXAMPLE

**165A @ 30% Duty Cycle**

> **[Duty cycle clock — diagram]** A clock/pie diagram divided to show the 10-minute duty-cycle period split between welding and resting time. Data: Left label "3 Minutes Welding" corresponds to the smaller shaded wedge (30% of the 10-minute period); right label "7 Minutes Resting" corresponds to the remaining 70% of the clock face. Example current: 165A at 30% duty cycle.
