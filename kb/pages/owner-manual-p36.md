# Wire Weld Penetration (Workpiece Heat Control)

> **[Weld penetration comparison — diagram (three profile views)]** Three cross-section (profile) illustrations of a weld bead through two joined plates, shown side by side for comparison. Data: **LEFT — "EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH": Weld droops on top and underneath, or falls through entirely, making a hole.** The profile shows the weld metal sunken/concave on the top surface and bulging far below the underside of the plate. **CENTER — "PROPER PENETRATION": Weld is visible underneath and bulges slightly on top.** The profile shows a tapered weld that slightly crowns above the top surface and just breaks through/protrudes slightly at the bottom. **RIGHT — "INADEQUATE PENETRATION": Weld does not penetrate the joint fully, just on the surface.** The profile shows a shallow lens of weld metal sitting only on the top surface, with an unfused vertical gap line running down between the two plates. The label "PROFILE VIEWS" appears beneath the center image. A bold arrow points from the left (excess penetration) image down to the left "POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS" list, and a bold arrow points from the right (inadequate penetration) image down to the right "POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS" list.

**EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH**
Weld droops on top and underneath, or falls through entirely, making a hole.

**PROPER PENETRATION**
Weld is visible underneath and bulges slightly on top.

**INADEQUATE PENETRATION**
Weld does not penetrate the joint fully, just on the surface.

*PROFILE VIEWS*

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS (Excess Penetration or Burn-Through)

1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
   Decrease weld current.
2. **Travel speed too slow:**
   Increase travel speed and ensure that travel speed is kept steady.
3. **Excessive material at weld:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS (Inadequate Penetration)

4. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Maintain 1/2" or less CTWD.
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold MIG Gun at proper angles.
5. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Reduce travel speed.
   Increase weld current.
6. **Workpieces too thick/close:**
   Bevel thick workpieces, allow slight gap, and weld in several passes.
7. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase wire feed speed.

# Bend at Joint

> **[Bend at joint — diagram (profile view)]** Cross-section illustration labeled "PROFILE VIEW" showing two plates joined by a weld bead where the plates have distorted upward/angled away from flat, forming a V-shaped bend centered on the weld. Data: the weld metal fills the joint at the apex of the bend, illustrating warpage caused by improper clamping or excessive heat.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Improper clamping:**
   Clamp workpieces securely.
   Make tack welds to hold workpieces.
2. **Excessive heat:**
   Weld a small portion and allow to cool before proceeding.
   Increase travel speed.
   Reduce wire feed speed.

# Coat of Slag Over Weld

> **[Slag over weld — photo/illustration (top view)]** Labeled "TOP VIEW", the image shows a weld bead running along a workpiece with a MIG gun nozzle at the right end. A dark coating of slag covers most of the bead, while a section has been chipped away to reveal the bright rippled weld underneath. Caption: *PARTIALLY CHIPPED AWAY TO SHOW WELD*.

*PARTIALLY CHIPPED AWAY TO SHOW WELD*

Slag is a necessary part of a flux-cored wire weld. It shields the weld from impurities. Clean off the slag with a Chipping Hammer and Wire Brush after welding.

Gas-shielded MIG welds are protected by the shielding gas and do not need slag to protect them.

# Weld Not Adhering Properly

Gaps present between weld and previous bead or between weld and workpiece. See areas below.

> **[Weld not adhering — diagram (profile view)]** Cross-section labeled "PROFILE VIEW" of a weld in a grooved/beveled joint. Data: dashed oval circles highlight the problem areas — gaps/lack of fusion at the upper left and upper right where the weld bead meets the sidewalls of the joint (between weld and previous bead/workpiece), and at the bottom root of the joint where the weld fails to fuse to the bottom of the piece.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Place stringer bead at correct place in joint.
   Adjust workpiece position or weld angle to permit proper welding to bottom of piece.
   Pause briefly at sides during weave bead.
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold MIG Gun at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Increase current.
   Increase wire feed speed.
3. **Dirty workpiece:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
4. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase wire feed speed.
5. **Workpiece gap too narrow:**
   Widen groove or increase bevel.
