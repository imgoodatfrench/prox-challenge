# Stick Weld–Coat of Slag Over Weld

> **[Stick Weld – Coat of Slag Over Weld — photo/illustration]** TOP VIEW of a stick weld bead shown mostly covered by a dark, opaque coat of slag; at the right end the slag has been broken open to reveal the rippled, stacked-crescent weld bead underneath, with the electrode/rod tip and a bright molten ball at the end of the bead. Caption beneath the image reads "PARTIALLY CHIPPED AWAY TO SHOW WELD". Data: The dark layer over the bead is the slag covering; the exposed ripples are the sound weld metal beneath it — slag is expected on a normal stick weld and simply needs removal.

*TOP VIEW*

**PARTIALLY CHIPPED AWAY TO SHOW WELD**

Slag is a necessary part of a stick weld.  It shields the weld from impurities.  Clean off the slag with the Chipping Hammer and Wire Brush after welding.

# Stick Weld–Porosity — Small cavities or holes in the bead.

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**

> **[Stick Weld – Porosity — illustration]** TOP VIEW of a stick weld bead with normal ripple pattern but speckled with numerous small, light-colored round cavities/pinholes scattered across the bead surface and into the molten ball at the end of the bead. Data: The defect signature is small white pits/holes distributed over the whole bead face, indicating gas porosity rather than a surface deposit.

*TOP VIEW*

1. **Dirty workpiece or fill material:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
   Make certain that fill material and Electrode are clean and free from oil, coatings, and other residues.
2. **Inconsistent welding speed:**
   Maintain steady weld speed.

# Stick Weld–Crooked/Wavy Bead

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**

> **[Stick Weld – Crooked/Wavy Bead — illustration]** TOP VIEW of a stick weld bead whose ripples wander from side to side instead of running in a straight line; the overlapping crescent ripples are offset up and down along the travel path, ending in a rounded molten ball. Data: The bead centerline snakes/meanders rather than staying straight, the visual signature of unsteady torch/electrode travel.

*TOP VIEW*

1. **Inaccurate welding:**
   Use two hands or rest hand on steady surface.
2. **Inconsistent welding speed:**
   Maintain steady weld speed.

# Stick Weld–Excessive Spatter

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**

> **[Stick Weld – Excessive Spatter — illustration]** TOP VIEW of a stick weld bead with a large number of coarse, dark, irregular metal droplets thrown onto the base metal on both sides of and along the bead. Data: The defect signature is grainy, large spatter particles surrounding the bead; fine spatter around a bead is normal.

*TOP VIEW*

Fine spatter is normal. Spatter that is grainy and large is a problem.

**Dirty workpiece or fill material:**
Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
Make certain that fill material and Electrode are clean and free from oil, coatings, and other residues.

# Stick Weld–Burn-Through — Base material melts away, leaving a hole in the weld.

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**

> **[Stick Weld – Burn-Through — illustration]** TOP VIEW of a stick weld bead in which the center of the bead has melted completely away, leaving a large open white (void) hole through the base material surrounded by a rim of melted metal; the rounded molten ball appears at the right end of the bead. Data: The defect signature is a hole opened through the joint where the base metal melted away, rather than a continuous bead.

*TOP VIEW*

1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce current.
2. **Welding speed too slow:**
   Increase welding speed and ensure that welding speed is kept steady.
3. **Excessive material at weld:**
   Reduce amount of fill material.
