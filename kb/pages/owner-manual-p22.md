# Basic Wire Welding Technique

1. Press (and hold) Trigger and contact the area to be welded with electrode wire to ignite arc.

2. For a narrow weld, you can usually draw the wire in a steady straight line.
   This is called a *stringer bead*.

   For a wider weld, draw the wire back and forth across the joint.
   This is called a *weave bead* and takes practice to perform properly.

> **[Bead Types — diagram]** Two shaded illustrations side by side comparing weld bead patterns. Data: Left panel labeled "stringer bead" shows a straight, uniform row of overlapping ripples with a single straight arrow pointing forward along the joint (steady straight-line travel). Right panel labeled "weave bead" shows a wider bead made of large overlapping loops with curved back-and-forth (side-to-side) arrows advancing along the joint, producing a wider deposit.

3. Direct the welding wire straight into the joint. This gives an angle of 90° (straight up and down) for butt (end to end) welds, and an angle of 45° for fillet (T-shaped) welds.

> **[Weld MIG Gun angles, viewed from front of weld joint — diagram]** Two gun-position illustrations viewed from the front of the joint. Data: Left illustration, "fillet weld joint" — a vertical plate meets a horizontal plate forming a T; the MIG gun is held tilted so the wire bisects the corner at a marked angle of **45°** into the joint root. Right illustration, "butt weld joint" — two plates meet end to end in the same plane; the MIG gun is held vertically (straight up and down) with the wire entering the joint at a marked angle of **90°** to the workpiece.

4. For MIG welding using solid wire and shielding gas, the end of the MIG Gun should be tilted so that wire is angled anywhere in-between straight on and 15° away from the direction you are welding. The amount of tilt is called the *push angle*.

5. When using flux-cored wire without shielding gas, the end of the MIG Gun should be tilted so that wire is angled anywhere in-between straight on and 15° in the direction you are welding. The amount of tilt is called the *drag angle*.

> **[Push Angle vs. Drag Angle — diagram]** Two side-by-side cutaway gun illustrations, each with a "Weld Direction" arrow pointing to the right over the workpiece and completed bead behind. Data: Left, titled "Push Angle," captioned "Solid Wire with Shielding Gas" — the gun is tilted so the nozzle leans back away from the travel direction, wire angled between straight on and **0–15°** (angle marked "0-15°" at the gun), pushing the puddle ahead of the gun. Right, titled "Drag Angle," captioned "Flux-Cored Wire without Gas" — the gun is tilted the opposite way, leaning into/toward the direction of travel, wire angled between straight on and **0–15°** (marked "0-15°"), dragging/trailing the puddle.

6. The Contact Tip should remain within 1/2" of the work surface. This distance is called *CTWD* – Contact Tip to Work Distance.

> **[CTWD — Contact Tip to Work Distance — diagram]** A single MIG gun shown angled down onto a workpiece with a deposited bead. Data: A dimension callout from the end of the contact tip down to the work surface is labeled "CTWD (up to 1/2")", indicating the stick-out/standoff distance between contact tip and work must not exceed 1/2 inch.
