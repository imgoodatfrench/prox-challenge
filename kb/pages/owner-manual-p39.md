# Stick Weld Penetration (Workpiece Heat Control)

| EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH | PROPER PENETRATION | INADEQUATE PENETRATION |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weld droops on top and underneath or falls through entirely, making a hole. | Weld is visible underneath and bulges slightly on top. | Weld does not contact the joint fully, just on the surface. |

> **[Stick Penetration Profile Views — diagram]** Three side-by-side cross-section (profile view) drawings of a butt joint between two plates, labeled *PROFILE VIEWS*. Data: (1) EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH — the dark weld metal sags below the bottom surface of the plates and the top of the weld is concave/dished, showing droop and a hole through the joint. (2) PROPER PENETRATION — dark weld metal fully fills the joint, crowns slightly above the top surface, and its root just reaches and is visible at the underside of the plates. (3) INADEQUATE PENETRATION — a small lens of weld metal sits only on the top surface of the joint; the vertical gap between the two plates remains open and unfused below it.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS FOR EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH

1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce current.
2. **Welding speed too slow:**
   Increase welding speed and ensure that welding speed is kept steady.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS FOR INADEQUATE PENETRATION

1. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold torch at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Slow down so fill material has time to melt into the weld location. Increase current.
3. **Workpieces too thick/close:**
   Bevel thick workpieces, allow slight gap, and weld in several passes.
4. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase amount of fill material.

# Stick Weld – Weld Not Adhering Properly

Gaps present between weld and previous bead or between weld and workpiece.  See areas below.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

> **[Weld Not Adhering — diagram]** Profile view cross-section of a narrow V/keyhole-shaped weld deposited in a joint between two plates. Data: dashed circles highlight the defect areas — two dashed circles at the upper left and upper right where the weld cap meets the top surface of the workpieces (lack of fusion to the previous bead/workpiece at the toes), and one dashed circle at the bottom of the weld root where the weld fails to fuse to the bottom of the joint. Label at left reads *PROFILE VIEW*.

1. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Place stringer bead at correct place in joint.
   Adjust workpiece position or weld angle to permit proper welding to bottom of piece.
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold Electrode and fill material at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Increase current.
3. **Dirty workpiece:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
4. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase amount of fill material.
5. **Distance between workpieces to large:**
   Decrease distance and increase bevel.

# Stick Weld – Bend at Joint

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

> **[Bend at Joint — diagram]** Profile view cross-section of two plates joined by a dark weld bead at the center. Data: the two workpieces are angled upward away from the weld (distorted/tilted), so the assembly forms a shallow V instead of lying flat — illustrating warping/bending at the joint caused by heat distortion or inadequate clamping. Label at left reads *PROFILE VIEW*.

1. **Improper clamping:**
   Clamp workpieces securely.
   Make tack welds to hold workpieces.
2. **Excessive heat:**
   Weld a small portion and allow to cool before proceeding.
   Increase weld speed.
