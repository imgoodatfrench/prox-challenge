# Wire Weld Diagnosis – Workpiece Heat Control / Weld Penetration

| INADEQUATE PENETRATION | PROPER PENETRATION | EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not hot enough | Ideal heat | Too hot |

> **[Penetration comparison — diagram]** Three cross-section views of a weld joint in plate, arranged left to right along a double-headed arrow scale labeled "Not hot enough" (left), "Ideal heat" (center), and "Too hot" (right). Data: LEFT — "INADEQUATE PENETRATION": a small lens-shaped bead sits on top of the joint, with the root gap/seam line still visible unfilled below it; weld metal does not reach through the plate. CENTER — "PROPER PENETRATION": a tapered, cone-shaped weld nugget extends from the top surface fully down through the plate thickness and protrudes slightly on the underside, fusing both plates. RIGHT — "EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH": the weld has melted a concave crater in the top surface and blown through the bottom of the plate, with molten metal bulging out the underside.

| How to increase workpiece heat and increase penetration:<br>(to weld THICKER workpieces properly) | How to reduce workpiece heat and limit penetration:<br>(to weld THINNER workpieces properly) |
| --- | --- |
| a. Increase weld current | e. Decrease weld current |
| b. Decrease travel speed | f. Increase travel speed |
| c. Use faster wire feed | g. Use slower wire feed |
| d. Use shorter CTWD | h. Use longer CTWD |

# Example Wire Weld Diagrams

> **[Example wire weld diagrams — diagram set]** Six panels, each showing a bead cross-section (top) over the plate and a top view of the bead along the joint (below), with the fault name and correction beneath. Data by panel, left to right: (1) **Good Weld** — cross-section shows a smooth, evenly crowned bead well fused to the plate; top view shows uniform, evenly spaced overlapping ripples of consistent width with a rounded crater at the end. (2) **Voltage Too Low or Wire Feed Too Slow** — cross-section shows a tall, narrow, ropy bead sitting high on the surface with little fusion; top view shows a narrow, high, stringy bead with widely spaced ripples. TO CORRECT: Increase Output Voltage or Increase Wire Feed Speed. (3) **Voltage Too High or Wire Feed Too Fast** — cross-section shows a wide, flat bead with undercut at the toes; top view shows a wide, flat, irregular bead with heavy spatter dots scattered on each side. TO CORRECT: Decrease Output Voltage or Decrease Wire Feed Speed. (4) **Travel Speed Too Fast** — cross-section shows a small, narrow, thin bead; top view shows a narrow, stretched bead with elongated pointed ripples and a small crater. TO CORRECT: illustrated by a gun held at an angle over the bead with an arrow pointing along the joint labeled "travel slower." (5) **Travel Speed Too Slow** — cross-section shows a wide, heavily built-up bead; top view shows a broad, flat, excessively wide deposit with a large rounded pool at the end. TO CORRECT: gun illustration with arrow labeled "travel faster." (6) **CTWD Too Long or Wrong Polarity** — cross-section shows a rounded bead with poor fusion; top view shows an irregular bead with porosity holes in the bead and heavy spatter scattered all around. TO CORRECT: Check Polarity and maintain less than 1/2" CTWD — illustrated by a gun held close and perpendicular to the workpiece with a downward arrow and a dimension line indicating the short contact-tip-to-work distance.
