# Troubleshooting–MIG/Flux-Cored Welding

**IMPORTANT!**

Be CERTAIN to shut off the Welder, disconnect it from power, and discharge the MIG Gun to ground before adjusting, cleaning, or repairing the unit.

| Problem | Possible Causes | Likely Solutions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wire Feed Motor Runs but Wire Does Not Feed Properly | 1. Insufficient wire feed pressure.<br>2. Incorrect wire feed roller size.<br>3. Damaged MIG Gun, cable, or liner assembly.<br>4. Feed Tensioner is too tight. | 1. Increase wire feed pressure properly. Follow step 27 on page 17.<br>2. Flip roll to correct size. Follow the Feed Roller instructions on page 12.<br>3. Have a qualified technician inspect these parts and replace as necessary.<br>4. Loosen Feed Tensioner so it applies only enough pressure to prevent continued spinning after the Gun Trigger is released. |
| Wire Creates a Bird's Nest During Operation | 1. Excess wire feed pressure.<br>2. Incorrect Contact Tip size.<br>3. MIG Gun Cable Connector not fully inserted into Wire Feed mechanism.<br>4. Damaged liner. | 1. Adjust wire feed pressure properly. Follow step 27 on page 17.<br>2. Replace with the proper tip for wire used.<br>3. Insert Gun Cable Connector properly. Follow steps 13 and 14 on page 13.<br>4. Have a qualified technician inspect and repair/replace as necessary. |
| Wire Stops During Welding | 1. Gun cable is severely bent and Wire Feed mechanism cannot feed wire.<br>2. Gun liner is clogged or worn.<br>3. Gun liner is too small for welding wire being used.<br>4. Wire is tangled on the spool.<br>5. Wire is not making contact with Feed Rollers.<br>6. Feed Roller is not making enough contact with wire or is crushing flux-cored wire. | 1. Straighten Gun cable.<br>2. Check gun liner for obstruction. Replace if necessary.<br>3. Check that gun liner is correct size for wire.<br>4. Check wire for cross winding or tangled spool.<br>5. Check Feed Rollers and ensure correct groove for wire diameter is being used.<br>6. Check Feed Tensioner and ensure it is set properly. |
| Welding Arc Not Stable | 1. Wire not feeding properly.<br>2. Incorrect Contact Tip or liner size or excessive wear.<br>3. Incorrect wire feed speed.<br>4. Loose MIG Gun cable or ground cable.<br>5. Damaged MIG Gun or loose connection within Gun.<br>6. Incorrect polarity for process being run.<br>7. Gas coverage may be insufficient or too high.<br>8. Poor connection with workpiece. | 1. See first Troubleshooting section above.<br>2. Replace with the proper tip or liner size for wire used.<br>3. Adjust wire feed speed to achieve a more stable arc.<br>4. Check to ensure that all connections are tight.<br>5. Have a qualified technician inspect and repair/replace as necessary.<br>6. Ensure polarity is correct for operation: DCEP for MIG welding and DCEN for Flux-Cored self-shielded welding.<br>7. Ensure gas flow rate is set according to Settings Chart. Make sure MIG Gun Cable Connector is fully inserted into Wire Feed mechanism with no O-Rings exposed.<br>8. Check the ground clamp connection to the workpiece and machine. Ensure the MIG Gun is properly secured. |
| Weak Arc Strength | 1. Incorrect line voltage.<br>2. Improper gauge or length of cord.<br>3. Not enough current. | 1. Check the line voltage and, if insufficient, have a licensed electrician remedy the situation.<br>2. Do not use an extension cord on this Welder. Use only one of the supplied power cords for this Welder or an identical replacement cord.<br>3. Switch current to proper setting for metal thickness. |
