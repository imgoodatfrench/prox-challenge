# Wire Weld−Porosity

**Small cavities or holes in the bead.**

> **[Porosity bead illustration — diagram]** Labeled "TOP VIEW"; top-down illustration of a wire weld bead made of overlapping stacked ripples with a rounded puddle at the leading end. Data: The bead face is dotted with numerous small light-colored round cavities/holes scattered across the ripples and in the leading puddle, depicting porosity in the finished bead.

## POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Incorrect polarity:**
   Check that polarity is set correctly
   for type of welding.
2. **Insufficient shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Increase flow of gas.
   Clean nozzle.
   Maintain proper CTWD.
3. **Incorrect shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Use shielding gas recommended by wire supplier.
4. **Dirty workpiece or welding wire:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
   Make certain that wire is clean and free
   from oil, coatings, and other residues.
5. **Inconsistent travel speed:**
   Maintain steady travel speed.
6. **CTWD too long:**
   Reduce CTWD.

# Wire Weld−Excessive Spatter

**Fine spatter is normal.**
**Spatter that is grainy and large is a problem.**

> **[Excessive spatter bead illustration — diagram]** Labeled "TOP VIEW"; top-down illustration of a wire weld bead of overlapping ripples with a rounded puddle at the leading end. Data: Numerous dark, irregular, grainy spatter specks of varying size are scattered on the base metal all around and along both sides of the bead, depicting excessive (large/grainy) spatter rather than fine spatter.

## POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Dirty workpiece or welding wire:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
   Make certain that wire is clean and free
   from oil, coatings, and other residues.
2. **Incorrect polarity:**
   Check that polarity is set correctly
   for type of welding.
3. **Insufficient shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Increase flow of gas.
   Clean nozzle.
   Maintain proper CTWD.
4. **Wire feeding too fast:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
5. **CTWD too long:**
   Reduce CTWD.

# Wire Weld−Crooked/Wavy Bead

> **[Crooked/wavy bead illustration — diagram]** Labeled "TOP VIEW"; top-down illustration of a wire weld bead whose overlapping ripples wander side to side rather than following a straight line, ending in a rounded puddle at the leading end. Data: The bead centerline is irregular and snake-like (crooked/wavy) instead of straight and uniform.

## POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Inaccurate welding:**
   Use two hands or rest hand on steady surface.
2. **Inconsistent travel speed:**
   Maintain steady travel speed.
3. **CTWD too long:**
   Reduce CTWD.

# Wire Weld−Burn-Through

**Base material melts away,**
**leaving a hole in the weld.**

> **[Burn-through bead illustration — diagram]** Labeled "TOP VIEW"; top-down illustration of a dark wire weld bead running across the base metal with a rounded puddle at the leading end. Data: A large white/open oval void is present in the middle of the bead where the base material has melted completely away, leaving a hole through the weld (burn-through).

## POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS

1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce current and/or wire feed speed.
2. **Travel speed too slow:**
   Increase travel speed and ensure
   that travel speed is kept steady.
3. **Excessive material at weld:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
